# DD-001: Mission

**Status:** Approved  
**Date:** 2026-08-07

## The Question

What is the purpose of the first cigar box guitar?

Before making detailed choices about construction, materials, dimensions, electronics or appearance, we need to understand what this instrument is intended to achieve.

## Goals

The reference instrument should:

- teach the principles of luthiery through practical application;
- provide an enjoyable and engaging design and build experience;
- result in an instrument that is rewarding to play;
- look, feel and sound sufficiently good that it establishes a meaningful quality benchmark;
- expose design decisions, trade-offs and lessons learned that can inform future instruments;
- provide a reference point from which future designs can evolve;
- help develop the workshop's design process through actual use rather than theoretical planning.

## Design Space

### Option 1: Experimental Prototype

Treat the first instrument primarily as a learning exercise.

The emphasis would be on speed, experimentation and discovering how the major components work together. Finish quality and long-term usefulness would be secondary.

**Advantages**

- encourages experimentation;
- lowers the cost of mistakes;
- allows rapid progress;
- maximises freedom to change direction.

**Disadvantages**

- may produce an instrument that is quickly superseded;
- provides a weak benchmark for future work;
- risks treating quality as something to pursue only later;
- does not fully test whether the process can produce an instrument people genuinely want to own.

### Option 2: Showcase Instrument

Treat the first instrument primarily as an attempt to produce the finest instrument possible.

The emphasis would be on appearance, finish, sound and playability.

**Advantages**

- establishes an ambitious quality target;
- encourages careful workmanship;
- could provide a compelling example of future work.

**Disadvantages**

- may discourage experimentation;
- creates pressure to avoid mistakes rather than learn from them;
- risks selecting familiar or conservative solutions simply because they appear safer;
- could make the build itself less enjoyable;
- focuses strongly on the destination at the expense of the learning journey.

### Option 3: Reference Instrument

Treat the first instrument as both a serious instrument and a learning platform.

It should be designed and built carefully enough to be genuinely desirable while deliberately exposing the principles, decisions and trade-offs involved in its creation.

**Advantages**

- balances learning with quality;
- allows experimentation without treating the instrument as disposable;
- creates a useful benchmark for future designs;
- tests both the instrument design process and the workshop process;
- gives discoveries from the first build somewhere useful to go next.

**Disadvantages**

- will take longer than a simple prototype;
- some decisions may require more investigation than strictly necessary to make a working instrument;
- the desire to learn and the desire to produce an exceptional instrument may occasionally compete.

## Trade-Offs

The central trade-off is between **learning through experimentation** and **producing a high-quality finished instrument**.

Optimising entirely for learning could result in a disposable prototype.

Optimising entirely for the finished product could discourage experimentation and turn the project into an exercise in avoiding mistakes.

The reference-instrument approach deliberately accepts some additional time and uncertainty in exchange for gaining both outcomes.

The build itself is also considered an important outcome. A technically successful instrument reached through an unnecessarily frustrating process would not fully satisfy the mission.

## Decision

**Option 3: Reference Instrument**

Build a reference cigar box guitar that teaches the principles of luthiery, delights both the builder and the player, and becomes the foundation for a family of future instruments.

## Consequences

This decision means that:

- learning value is a legitimate consideration in future design decisions;
- the enjoyment of the design and build process matters alongside the quality of the finished instrument;
- quality should not be deliberately sacrificed merely because this is the first build;
- experimentation is encouraged where it increases understanding;
- important decisions and discoveries should be recorded so they can inform later instruments;
- the completed instrument should remain useful as a physical reference against which future instruments can be compared;
- the workshop process itself will be tested and refined during the project.

## Future Revisit

The mission should remain stable throughout the reference build.

It should be revisited after the instrument has been completed and played for a meaningful period.

At that point we should ask:

- Did the project genuinely teach the principles we wanted to understand?
- Was the design and build process enjoyable?
- Does the finished instrument delight the player?
- Is it a useful benchmark for future instruments?
- What should the mission of the next instrument preserve, change or extend?

## Sign-Off

**Approved.**

This mission establishes the purpose against which subsequent design decisions for the reference cigar box guitar will be judged.
